Character Story & Description

Thrain Eisenkolben

Overview

Thrain Eisenkolben is a dwarf mutant and sworn warden: a loyal, calculating warrior who treats every fight like a siege plan. He is not charming (Charisma 8), and he does not try to be. What he offers instead is preparation, endurance, and a word once given that he will not break. He studies monsters the way a siege engineer studies walls — patiently, methodically, and with the intent to bring them down.

Backstory

The hold had been quiet for a long time before Thrain was born. Fewer births each generation, fewer hands to work the stone, and the dark pressing in from every side with more monsters than the clan could answer. By the time he came of age, the question was no longer whether the clan would survive, but how long it could hold. Thrain was already the hold's alchemist and a competent fighter, and he looked at the numbers the way he looked at a recipe: plainly, without sentiment, and with an eye for what was missing. What was missing was a warrior who could take far more punishment than any dwarf should and keep standing. So he volunteered for the hold's experimental alchemical mutation program. He was not the first to volunteer. He was the one who survived it intact enough to walk back out.

The mutations are the price written on his body, and he does not pretend otherwise. Spines ridged along his spine make grappling him a losing proposition. Eyes that catch torchlight a little too well see a hundred and twenty feet into the dark. A body that needs only an hour of true sleep to function, and a stance that lets him fight from the ground and rise in an instant. A surge of healing pulled from somewhere deeper than muscle. He hides most of it under dwarven-forged mail and a braided beard, but the marks are there for anyone who knows what to look for — and Thrain has learned to read marks on other people, too.

When the invasion came, he did exactly what he had been made for. He took his position at the gate and held it. Mutagen coursed through him, spines bared, hammer swinging in a steady, measured rhythm, while the clan fled into the tunnels behind him. He counted the dead as he made them — forty-two — because counting was the only way to keep his mind on the work while his body broke down around him. Eventually they brought him down: overwhelmed, knocked to the stone, dragged off in chains. The clan escaped. The tunnels swallowed them in the chaos, and contact was lost. He does not know whether they survived. He assumes they did, because assuming otherwise is not useful, and because his oath was to them, not to his own certainty.

He was held by the invaders until a band of adventurers broke in and pulled him out. He joined them — not from gratitude alone, but because a warden without a hold needs a garrison, and they were it. For years he was their planner, their night watch, their last line. They died, one by one or all at once; he does not dress it up or make it prettier than it was. He buried what he could and kept moving. The hold may still stand or may not; either way, the oath did not end when the hold did. He still keeps watch through the night — Nocturnal makes that literal — still reads stonework for the story it tells, and still carries a field journal of monster notes, recipes, and mutagen observations so that the knowledge outlives him.

Here he is: still counting, still watching the dark, still holding the line for whoever stands beside him. He attaches himself to a party the way a siege engineer attaches to a garrison — planning the retreats, rationing the supplies, making sure someone is always watching the dark. He does not seek glory. He seeks to keep the people beside him alive long enough to finish the job. And if the count of enemies must be kept again, he will keep it.

Personality & Mannerisms

Oath-bound & Loyal

Thrain's loyalty is not warm or effusive; it is structural. Once he has committed to a person or a cause, he treats that commitment as load-bearing. He will not abandon allies, and he will not break a promise, even when it costs him. He shows care through preparation — rations, bandages, smelling salts, curatives — rather than through words.

Calculating & Methodical

He plans before he acts. Mutagen selection, terrain, initiative order, and which Hit Die to spend are all variables he weighs deliberately. He prefers control (Trip Attack, Disarming Attack, fog and freeze) over slaughter, and would rather win a fight that never turns desperate. Long explanations do not bore him, but careless ones irritate him.

Gruff & Blunt

Low Charisma is not a flaw he resents; it is simply how he is. He speaks plainly, sometimes brusquely, and has little patience for flattery or small talk. His humor, when it appears, is dry and precise. He is not unkind — he is just unwilling to perform warmth he does not feel.

Fearless, Not Reckless

Thrain does not charge in without thought. He assesses the odds, decides they are acceptable, and then does not flinch. His courage is the courage of someone who has already priced in the worst case — and who has stared down an invasion force alone at a gate and kept counting. Adapted Resistance is taken as Frightened: fear is a variable he has learned to master, not ignore.

Appearance

Dwarven-forged mail worn over a hardened breastplate, with spiked pauldrons, alchemical etching, and a reinforced gauntlet; a round shield bears the same etching. A braided beard, alchemical vials at the belt, and a field journal at the hip. Beneath the mail, the mutation marks are visible to those who know to look: ridged scarring along the spine where the spines sit, eyes that catch torchlight a little too well in the dark, and a stance that is slightly too low and balanced for a dwarf of his build. He moves like someone who has learned to fight from the ground up.

Combat Style

Thrain fights like a warden holding a gate. He chooses one mutagen to match the threat (Precision for a focused target, Endurance for a war of attrition, Power for a short brutal exchange), keeps his shield readied for AC 18, and stows it only when he needs a free hand for alchemical substances or the two-handed warhammer grip. He uses Trip Attack and Disarming Attack to control rather than simply damage, and spends Hit Dice through Reserves of Strength and Dwarf Fortitude as a resource to be managed, not squandered. Fury turns a hit he takes into a hit he returns. He is at his most dangerous when someone has just hurt an ally — or him.

Recorded decisions

Adapted Resistance (level 9): Frightened — advantage on saves against effects that inflict Frightened and on saves to end it. Locked in to match the fearless-warden theme. Hardy already covers poison saves, so Poisoned was not taken.
